Database views

A view is a way of looking at a database. Add as many named views as you need; each keeps its own layout, filters, sorts and grouping, and every one of them shows the same records, so nothing is ever copied or out of date.

Ten ways to see the same work

Table
A keyboard-editable grid with a sticky header and first column, resizable columns and a bulk bar for selected rows.
Board
Cards in columns by status or any select property. Drag a card to another column to change it.
List
A compact list for reading down a long collection.
Gallery
Cards previewing each record's first image, or the start of its text, in small or large sizes. Good for moodboards, references and reading lists.
Calendar
Records on their dates. Drag to move a date; schedule undated records from the list below.
Timeline
Records as bars across time, for plans, sprints and milestones.
Feed
Records in a stream under day headings (Today, Yesterday, then dates), each with the start of its text and first image, like a journal or log.
Chart
Bar, line, area, pie, donut or a single number, computed live from the records.
Form
A form that collects entries into the database, one record per submission.
Canvas
A freeform board where record cards, notes and images go anywhere and join with labelled arrows. See Canvases.

Columns follow the order of the grouping property's options and show their counts. Cards show the properties you choose. Each card also has a small calendar handle: drag it onto a Day or Week calendar to plan a work session for that task without touching its deadline.

Switching and shaping views

Views sit in a tab strip above the database; the underline slides to the one you pick. The toolbar beside them holds the view's layout, filter, sort and property controls, with a count badge when a filter or sort is active and the number of records at the far right. Duplicate a view to try a variation without losing the original; a duplicated canvas keeps its layout.

Grouping

Table, board and list views group by a property, with one level of subgroups. Group headers fold away and show their counts, so a board by status can be split again by assignee area or priority.

Views inside pages

A Database view block shows a database inside any page, with its own view settings. A project's page can show only its open tasks as a board, while the full table lives elsewhere.
